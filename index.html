CAPSTONE PROJECT

Embedding the Counterfactual: Causal Inference for Environmental Policy with Satellite Representations

Solving the identification problem when latent ecological structure determines treatment assignment

Status Quo: Naïve Matching

[Visualization: Buffer-based control selection]

Conventional policy evaluation selects control units based on geographic proximity or outcome trends—ignoring latent ecological structure that determines both treatment assignment and outcomes.

Identification failure: When environmental heterogeneity is unobserved, spatial proximity does not guarantee comparability. Unobserved confounding invalidates causal claims.

Innovation: Embedding-Based Similarity

[Visualization: Embedding-based donor pool]

Embedding-based donor selection uses high-dimensional satellite representations (from foundation models like Clay, Prithvi, or Panopticon) to match treated sites to structurally similar counterfactuals—even when they are geographically distant.

Methodological contribution: Operationalizes latent similarity through representation learning, enabling valid synthetic control and causal forest estimation under spatial confounding.

The Problem

Environmental policy evaluation suffers from a fundamental identification problem: treatment assignment is rarely random. Regions selected for water conservation policies, conservation programs, or climate adaptation interventions differ systematically from control regions along dimensions that are difficult to observe—soil moisture dynamics, vegetation phenology, hydrological connectivity, microclimatic variation.

Traditional approaches—geographic buffers, propensity score matching on observables, or difference-in-differences with parallel trends assumptions—fail when latent confounding is severe and spatial interference is present.

The Identification Failure

Selecting control units based on proximity assumes that nearby regions are comparable. But in environmental contexts, this assumption is violated when:

  • Treatment assignment is based on unobserved biophysical suitability
  • Spillover effects propagate through hydrological or ecological networks
  • Time-varying environmental shocks affect treated and control units differently

These violations lead to selection bias and invalid counterfactual inference.

The Methodological Innovation

I propose using satellite-derived embeddings from foundation models (pre-trained on billions of Earth observation images) to construct a high-dimensional similarity space that captures latent ecological structure.

By projecting treated and potential control units into this embedding space, I can:

  • Identify structurally similar counterfactuals that are ecologically comparable to treated units—even if they are geographically distant
  • Construct synthetic controls that are weighted combinations of these embedding-matched donors
  • Apply causal forests to estimate heterogeneous treatment effects conditional on learned representations
  • Evaluate treatment effect transportability by measuring embedding-space distances between source and target populations

Why This Matters for Political Methodology

This approach addresses core challenges in observational causal inference:

  • Unobserved confounding: Embeddings capture latent factors that traditional covariates miss
  • High-dimensional matching: Avoids curse of dimensionality by using learned representations
  • External validity: Embedding distances formalize the concept of "similarity" required for generalization
  • Scalability: Enables policy evaluation at planetary scale using satellite data

Why This Matters for Environmental Governance

Policy impact: Enables rigorous evaluation of water conservation, climate adaptation, and land management policies where randomized trials are infeasible. Provides decision-makers with credible estimates of treatment effects and clear guidance on where policies are likely to succeed or fail.

Institutional relevance: Supports evidence-based policymaking by connecting methodological rigor to actionable insights for environmental governance.

METHODOLOGICAL TOOLKIT

Causal Inference & Geospatial Methods

Each method addresses a specific identification problem in observational policy evaluation

Synthetic Control Methods

Constructs counterfactual outcomes by weighting combinations of untreated units to match pre-treatment characteristics of treated units.

Solves: Missing counterfactual when only one or few units are treated and randomization is impossible.

Causal Forests

Machine learning approach to estimate heterogeneous treatment effects without parametric assumptions, using recursive partitioning.

Solves: Treatment effect heterogeneity when functional form is unknown and effects vary across subpopulations.

Difference-in-Differences

Compares changes in outcomes over time between treated and control groups, leveraging panel structure to control for time-invariant confounders.

Solves: Selection on unobservables that are time-invariant, using parallel trends assumption.

Bayesian Hierarchical Models

Multilevel regression with partial pooling, allowing effects to vary across groups while borrowing strength across units.

Solves: Small sample sizes within subgroups and uncertainty quantification in policy evaluation.

Representation Learning for Matching

Uses deep learning embeddings from satellite imagery to construct similarity metrics for donor selection in causal inference.

Solves: Latent confounding and high-dimensional covariate spaces in spatial contexts.

Policy Transportability Analysis

Evaluates external validity of treatment effects by assessing similarity between source and target populations using formal selection diagrams.

Solves: Generalization of policy effects from one context to another when populations differ.

Spatial Confounding Adjustment

Decomposes spatial variation into confounding and treatment effect components using restricted spatial regression or Gaussian processes.

Solves: Biased treatment effect estimates when spatially-structured confounders exist.

Instrumental Variables

Uses exogenous variation in treatment assignment to estimate causal effects when treatment is endogenous.

Solves: Omitted variable bias and simultaneity when confounders are unobserved or unmeasured.

Applied Causal Inference for Policy Evaluation

Policy Transportability in Agricultural Water Management

Context: Vietnam → Japan
Policy: Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) Irrigation
Methods: Counterfactual Simulation, Spatial Heterogeneity, Institutional Analysis

Research Question: Under what conditions can a water conservation policy proven effective in one country be successfully transferred to another?

Identification Challenge: AWD adoption depends on biophysical suitability (soil permeability, rainfall patterns, terrain) and institutional constraints (farmer information, subsidy availability, agricultural extension systems). Simply observing AWD success in Vietnam does not guarantee transportability to Japan.

Approach: Constructed satellite-derived biophysical suitability indices (CHIRPS rainfall, MODIS evapotranspiration, SMAP soil moisture, SoilGrids, NASADEM) and developed counterfactual water balance simulations (AQUAWAT model) to model heterogeneous treatment effects across environmental contexts. Conducted comparative institutional analysis to identify political economy barriers: farmer adoption constraints, extension service capacity, and subsidy structures.

Causal Inference Framework: Treatment effect heterogeneity estimated using covariate-specific counterfactual simulations. Transportability assessed by comparing distributions of effect modifiers (soil type, precipitation deficit) between Vietnam and Japan.

Policy Impact: Provided actionable suitability maps and adoption guidelines for Japanese Ministry of Agriculture. Identified regions where AWD would succeed (low permeability soils, moderate rainfall) vs. fail (high permeability, extreme drought risk). Quantified institutional barriers requiring policy intervention.

Trade Policy Incidence and Supply Chain Diffusion

Context: China-Vietnam Trade
Policy: Anti-Dumping Tariffs on Chinese Hot-Rolled Steel
Methods: Institutional Power Analysis, Quantitative Incidence Modeling

Research Question: How do anti-dumping tariffs create unintended market responses, and how does institutional power shape compliance behavior?

Policy Context: Vietnam imposed anti-dumping tariffs on Chinese hot-rolled coil (HRC) under WTO Article VI framework. Examined 10 case studies under Part 20 procedures.

Theoretical Framework: Power and information asymmetries allow large firms to negotiate tariff exceptions. Policy incidence is determined not just by tariff rates but by institutional capacity to enforce compliance and firm bargaining power.

Quantitative Analysis: Modeled tariff diffusion through supply chains: Chinese HRC tariffs → higher input costs for Vietnamese importers → domestic mills gain market share → downstream consumption declines. Estimated price elasticities and market share shifts using trade data and firm-level responses.

Identification Strategy: Compared outcomes for firms that received tariff exemptions vs. those that did not, controlling for firm size, industry, and political connections. Assessed whether exemptions were driven by economic efficiency or rent-seeking.

Findings: Large firms successfully lobbied for exemptions, undermining policy intent. Tariff incidence fell disproportionately on small importers. Domestic steel mills gained short-term protection but downstream industries faced higher costs.

Contribution to Political Methodology: Demonstrates how institutional analysis and quantitative modeling must be integrated to understand policy effects. Trade policy cannot be evaluated without understanding compliance mechanisms and power dynamics.

Bayesian Hierarchical Modeling for Fiscal Policy Evaluation

Context: Argentina Government Finance
Focus: Revenue and Wage Expenditure Dynamics
Methods: Bayesian Regression, Uncertainty Quantification

Research Question: How do government revenue streams and wage expenditures respond to macroeconomic shocks and policy changes?

Statistical Approach: Applied Bayesian hierarchical regression (PyMC) to model the relationship between revenue sources (tax income, commodity exports) and public sector wage expenses. Allowed coefficients to vary by province and time period, capturing heterogeneity in fiscal capacity.

Why Bayesian? Small sample sizes at the provincial level and high uncertainty in revenue projections require principled uncertainty quantification. Bayesian framework allows incorporation of prior information (from historical data or expert judgment) and propagates uncertainty through the model to policy recommendations.

Identification: Controlled for endogeneity of wage policy (governments adjust wages in response to revenue shocks) by instrumenting revenue using exogenous commodity price shocks.

Policy Relevance: Provided probabilistic forecasts of fiscal sustainability under alternative revenue scenarios. Identified provinces at high risk of fiscal crisis. Estimated the effect of wage freezes on expenditure reduction.

Methodological Contribution: Demonstrates the value of Bayesian methods for policy evaluation when data are limited and uncertainty is high. Interactive visualization (Streamlit app) allows policymakers to explore scenarios.

TECHNICAL CAPABILITIES

Geospatial Machine Learning & Remote Sensing

Extracting latent socioeconomic and ecological structure from satellite imagery using deep learning

Representation Learning from Earth Observation Data

Objective: Extract high-dimensional embeddings from satellite imagery to capture latent features (urbanization density, vegetation health, infrastructure development, socioeconomic status) that are not directly observable but are critical for policy evaluation.

Application to Political Methodology: These embeddings serve as learned confounders in causal inference pipelines. By using foundation models (pre-trained on billions of satellite images), I can construct similarity metrics that capture complex environmental and social contexts—enabling more credible counterfactual estimation.

Architecture & Models

  • Foundation Models: Panopticon, Clay, Prithvi (satellite-specific transformers)
  • Vision Transformers (ViT): Self-attention for spatial context
  • CNNs: ResNet, U-Net for segmentation and feature extraction
  • Embedding Techniques: Contrastive learning, SimCLR, BYOL

Data Sources & Preprocessing

  • Satellite Platforms: Landsat 5/8, Sentinel-2, MODIS
  • Biophysical Layers: CHIRPS (rainfall), SMAP (soil moisture), NASADEM (elevation)
  • Preprocessing: Atmospheric correction, cloud masking, time-series aggregation, spatial alignment
  • Tools: Google Earth Engine, GDAL, Rasterio, PyTorch

Downstream Causal Tasks

  • Donor selection for synthetic control
  • Covariate adjustment in regression
  • Heterogeneous treatment effect estimation (causal forests)
  • Spatial confounding adjustment

Example: Evaluating Georgia Water Policy

Planetary Causal Inference Lab: Curated pre-treatment Landsat 5 imagery, extracted embeddings using Panopticon foundation model, and benchmarked against CNN, ViT, and Clay models. Used embeddings in CausalImagesR to evaluate treatment effect heterogeneity of Georgia's water conservation policy.

Why This Matters: Satellite-derived embeddings operationalize the concept of "latent similarity" required for credible causal inference in spatial contexts. This connects cutting-edge computer vision research to core problems in political methodology.
