METHODOLOGICAL TOOLKIT

Causal Inference & Geospatial Methods

Each method addresses a specific identification problem in observational policy evaluation

Synthetic Control Methods

Constructs counterfactual outcomes by weighting combinations of untreated units to match pre-treatment characteristics of treated units.

Solves: Missing counterfactual when only one or few units are treated and randomization is impossible.

Causal Forests

Machine learning approach to estimate heterogeneous treatment effects without parametric assumptions, using recursive partitioning.

Solves: Treatment effect heterogeneity when functional form is unknown and effects vary across subpopulations.

Difference-in-Differences

Compares changes in outcomes over time between treated and control groups, leveraging panel structure to control for time-invariant confounders.

Solves: Selection on unobservables that are time-invariant, using parallel trends assumption.

Bayesian Hierarchical Models

Multilevel regression with partial pooling, allowing effects to vary across groups while borrowing strength across units.

Solves: Small sample sizes within subgroups and uncertainty quantification in policy evaluation.

Representation Learning for Matching

Uses deep learning embeddings from satellite imagery to construct similarity metrics for donor selection in causal inference.

Solves: Latent confounding and high-dimensional covariate spaces in spatial contexts.

Policy Transportability Analysis

Evaluates external validity of treatment effects by assessing similarity between source and target populations using formal selection diagrams.

Solves: Generalization of policy effects from one context to another when populations differ.

Spatial Confounding Adjustment

Decomposes spatial variation into confounding and treatment effect components using restricted spatial regression or Gaussian processes.

Solves: Biased treatment effect estimates when spatially-structured confounders exist.

Instrumental Variables

Uses exogenous variation in treatment assignment to estimate causal effects when treatment is endogenous.

Solves: Omitted variable bias and simultaneity when confounders are unobserved or unmeasured.

Biophysical suitability improves in regions with moderate rainfall (50-70mm deficit) and lower permeability soils. Extreme drought zones (>100mm deficit) pose high crop failure risk.

Political Economy Barriers

  • Extension Service Gaps: Japan's agricultural extension lacks AWD training vs. Vietnam's decade-long farmer education
  • Subsidy Misalignment: Japanese subsidies favor water-intensive crops; AWD incentives absent
  • Farm Structure: Smaller Japanese farms have less flexibility than Vietnamese collectives for policy adoption
  • Information Asymmetry: Farmers skeptical of unproven water-saving claims absent local evidence

Actionable Recommendations

✓ High-Priority Zones: Focus AWD adoption in low-permeability regions with 50-70mm deficit threshold (≈28% of paddy area)

✓ Institutional Requirements: Bundle policy with extension training and initial subsidies to offset adoption costs

✗ Low-Feasibility Zones: Avoid implementation in high-permeability or extreme drought regions despite policy enthusiasm

Transportability Assessment: AWD success contingent on institutional capacity building, not biophysical suitability alone.

Why Tariffs Fail: The Compliance Problem

Finding: Large firms successfully lobbied for tariff exemptions (≈35% of imports), undermining policy intent and creating a two-tiered market.

Mechanism: Information asymmetry + firm bargaining power allow large importers to negotiate exemptions on claims of "efficiency." Small importers without political connections face full tariff, creating market concentration.

Policy Implication: Trade policy effectiveness depends on institutional capacity to enforce compliance, not tariff rates alone. Exemption requests reveal hidden bargaining dynamics that quantitative trade models miss.

TECHNICAL CAPABILITIES

Geospatial Machine Learning & Remote Sensing

Extracting latent socioeconomic and ecological structure from satellite imagery using deep learning

Representation Learning from Earth Observation Data

Objective: Extract high-dimensional embeddings from satellite imagery to capture latent features (urbanization density, vegetation health, infrastructure development, socioeconomic status) that are not directly observable but are critical for policy evaluation.

Application to Political Methodology: These embeddings serve as learned confounders in causal inference pipelines. By using foundation models (pre-trained on billions of satellite images), I can construct similarity metrics that capture complex environmental and social contexts—enabling more credible counterfactual estimation.

Architecture & Models

  • Foundation Models: Panopticon, Clay, Prithvi (satellite-specific transformers)
  • Vision Transformers (ViT): Self-attention for spatial context
  • CNNs: ResNet, U-Net for segmentation and feature extraction
  • Embedding Techniques: Contrastive learning, SimCLR, BYOL

Data Sources & Preprocessing

  • Satellite Platforms: Landsat 5/8, Sentinel-2, MODIS
  • Biophysical Layers: CHIRPS (rainfall), SMAP (soil moisture), NASADEM (elevation)
  • Preprocessing: Atmospheric correction, cloud masking, time-series aggregation, spatial alignment
  • Tools: Google Earth Engine, GDAL, Rasterio, PyTorch

Downstream Causal Tasks

  • Donor selection for synthetic control
  • Covariate adjustment in regression
  • Heterogeneous treatment effect estimation (causal forests)
  • Spatial confounding adjustment

Example: Evaluating Georgia Water Policy

Planetary Causal Inference Lab: Curated pre-treatment Landsat 5 imagery, extracted embeddings using Panopticon foundation model, and benchmarked against CNN, ViT, and Clay models. Used embeddings in CausalImagesR to evaluate treatment effect heterogeneity of Georgia's water conservation policy.

Why This Matters: Satellite-derived embeddings operationalize the concept of "latent similarity" required for credible causal inference in spatial contexts. This connects cutting-edge computer vision research to core problems in political methodology.
